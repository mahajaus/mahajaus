THE CATCH WITH XAUUSD ON MT5/EXNESS

A real footprint needs tick-by-tick trades with an aggressor side (who hit the
ask vs. who hit the bid). Spot gold CFDs have no central exchange, so MT5 gives
you *tick volume* — the number of price updates, not contracts traded — and no
bid/ask split at all. Any "footprint" drawn on XAUUSDm is reconstructed from
broker quote flow, which is your broker's feed, not the market's. Delta from it
is noise dressed as information.

WHERE THE REAL GOLD ORDER FLOW LIVES

COMEX GC futures (and MGC micro). CME publishes actual traded volume with
aggressor side, and XAUUSD tracks GC almost tick for tick. The professional
pattern is: read footprint/volume profile on GC, execute on XAUUSDm where your
spreads and lot sizing are better. That's a legitimate edge — you're seeing the
volume your broker's feed hides.

WHAT IT WOULD ACTUALLY ADD

Your bots are indicator-driven (EMA, RSI, ATR, MTF). Footprint answers a
different question — not *where* price is, but *whether the move has
participation behind it*:

  - Absorption — heavy volume at a level with price refusing to move = someone
    big is defending it. Your best pullback entries sit right there.
  - Delta divergence — price makes a new high, delta doesn't. Classic
    exhaustion, and a good veto filter on late entries.
  - High/low volume nodes — HVNs act as magnets and stall zones, LVNs get
    traversed fast. Better SL/TP placement than pure ATR multiples.

My honest read: this belongs as a *context and veto layer*, not a signal
generator. Footprint reading is heavily discretionary and notoriously hard to
automate — most people who try end up curve-fitting delta thresholds. If you
want to test it, the cheapest path is a Rithmic or CQG data feed into something
like ATAS or Sierra Chart on GC, manually logged against your existing signals
for a month. If footprint context would have filtered out losers, then think
about codifying it.

Taking the aggregator, since the hypothesis has nothing to read from until it

INPUT SCHEMA — BINANCE AGGTRADES

Each row: aggregate trade ID, price, quantity, first trade ID, last trade ID,
timestamp (milliseconds, UTC), isBuyerMaker, and a best-price-match flag.

isBuyerMaker is the field the whole system hinges on, and it is
counterintuitive. When it's TRUE, the buyer was resting passively — so the trade
was hit by an aggressive SELLER. That volume goes in your SELL/BID column. When
FALSE, the aggressor was the buyer, and it goes in the BUY/ASK column. Getting
this backwards inverts every delta reading in the system and produces a bot that
looks like it works and is exactly wrong. Write this mapping down and test it
explicitly.

CONFIG SURFACE

Bar interval, price tick size, imbalance ratio threshold, minimum volume floor
for imbalance eligibility, value area percentage. All of these become
sensitivity-test knobs later, so make them parameters from day one rather than
constants sprinkled through the code.

PROCESSING STAGES

  1. Bar assignment — floor timestamp to the interval. Use left-closed,
     right-open boundaries and stay in UTC throughout. Mixing timezone
     conventions is the second most common source of silent corruption here.
  2. Price bucketing — floor price to the tick. Do this in integer arithmetic,
     not floats; float rounding will scatter volume across adjacent buckets at
     the boundaries and quietly distort your ladder.
  3. Volume split — for each (bar, price level), sum quantity into
     buy-aggressor and sell-aggressor columns per the flag mapping above.

OUTPUT SCHEMA

A long-format table: bar_start, price_level, buy_volume, sell_volume.
Everything else is derived from this, so keep it raw and denormalize
downstream.

DERIVED METRICS

  - Level delta — buy minus sell at each price.
  - Bar delta — sum across the bar. Cumulative delta — running sum across bars.
  - POC — the price level with the highest total volume in the bar.
  - Value area — expand outward from POC until you've enclosed 70% of bar
    volume.
  - Imbalance — the one people implement wrong. It's conventionally DIAGONAL,
    not horizontal: compare buy volume at price P against sell volume at price
    P minus one tick. Flag when the ratio exceeds your threshold AND both sides
    clear the minimum volume floor. Without that floor, thin levels generate
    constant fake imbalances.
  - Stacked imbalance — three or more consecutive flagged levels in the same
    direction. This is the signal most order-flow strategies actually key on.

THE VALIDATION GATE

Pick one specific day and one specific 5-minute bar. Compare your POC, bar
delta, and per-level splits against the same bar in a free footprint tool. They
should match to the decimal. If they don't, stop and reconcile — do not proceed
to strategy logic. Roughly every failed order-flow project I'd expect you to
encounter skipped this step.

Do the hypothesis next — but not the way most people do it. Don't start with a
strategy. Start with a signal-existence test.

STEP ONE: DOES THE METRIC CARRY ANY FORWARD INFORMATION AT ALL?

Before you write entry rules, stops, or sizing, run a conditional-return study
on your aggregator output. It's a few lines once the table exists:

  - Take every bar in your month of data. Compute the baseline: mean and median
    forward return over the next N bars (try N of 3, 12, 48), plus the
    distribution.
  - Now split bars into buckets — bars containing a stacked bid imbalance, bars
    containing a stacked ask imbalance, bars with neither.
  - Compare each bucket's forward-return distribution against the baseline.

If stacked imbalances carry information, those distributions separate. If they
don't, the buckets look like the baseline with noise, and you've learned in an
afternoon what would otherwise take you six months of strategy iteration to
discover. Repeat for delta divergence and for closes outside the value area.

This is the highest-value thing you can do, because it's the question every
strategy silently assumes the answer to.

STEP TWO: ONLY IF SOMETHING SEPARATES, WRITE THE HYPOTHESIS AROUND IT.

And write it as one sentence with numbers in it — direction, trigger, horizon,
and expected hit rate. If a bucket showed edge, the hypothesis is just that
bucket with entry mechanics bolted on.

KILL CRITERIA — DECIDE THESE NOW, BEFORE YOU SEE ANY OUTPUT.

Write them into the repo as a text file today:

  - If no bucket's median forward return separates from baseline by a
    meaningful margin across all three horizons, the metric is dead and I stop.
  - If separation exists in one month but vanishes in a different month, it's
    noise and I stop.
  - I get three threshold configurations to test. Not thirty.

That third one is the important one. Order flow has a strong mythology around it
and thresholds are continuous, which means you can always find a setting that
looks good on data you've already seen. Committing to a small, pre-declared
search space is most of what stands between a real result and an elaborate
self-deception.

Practical sequence: aggregator -> validation against a reference chart ->
conditional-return study -> hypothesis -> backtest. You're two steps from
knowing whether this project is worth building at all, and neither step requires
a broker, a live feed, or a dollar.

Next is the backtest harness — and for a perp, execution realism is where most
backtests go from promising to worthless.

STRUCTURAL RULES

Event-driven, bar by bar, no vectorized shortcuts. At bar i the engine may only
read data through bar i's close. The classic lookahead bug here is subtle: your
imbalance flags are computed FROM the bar, so a signal on bar i can only be
acted on at bar i+1's open. If you enter at bar i's close you're trading on
information you didn't have, and the equity curve will look wonderful.

COST MODEL — THE PART PEOPLE SKIP

BTCUSDT perp has three costs, and together they'll eat a marginal edge alive:

  - Taker fees — roughly 4–5 bps per side on Binance perps at base tier. An
    order-flow bot is almost always a taker. Round-trip that's ~10 bps, which on
    a scalping horizon can exceed your entire gross edge.
  - Funding — charged every 8 hours to whichever side is crowded. If your
    strategy holds positions across funding stamps, model it explicitly. It's
    small per event and large in aggregate.
  - Slippage — assume at minimum one tick against you on entry and exit. Since
    you have the full trade tape, you can do better: estimate realized slippage
    from the actual volume at the touched levels.

Build the harness so you can run it with costs off and costs on. The gap between
those two equity curves tells you how much fragility you're carrying.

DATA DISCIPLINE

Split before you look. Take your month for development, then hold a completely
separate month — ideally from a different volatility regime — that you do not
touch until you have a final candidate. One look at the holdout and it's no
longer a holdout. If the strategy survives, walk it forward across several
months rather than testing on one aggregated blob.

METRICS THAT ACTUALLY DECIDE IT

Win rate is the least informative number and the most seductive. Look at
expectancy per trade net of costs, maximum drawdown, and above all TRADE COUNT
— a 65% win rate over 40 trades is statistically indistinguishable from luck.
You want hundreds of trades before you believe anything, which is another reason
24/7 crypto was the right call.

WHERE THE SURPRISES USUALLY LAND: costs turn a winner into a loser, or
performance concentrates in a handful of trades. Check the second one by
deleting your five best trades and re-reading the curve. If the edge disappears
with them, you don't have a strategy — you have five lucky days.

That's the full pipeline: data -> aggregator -> validation -> signal-existence
test -> hypothesis -> backtest with realistic costs -> holdout. Everything after
that is execution engineering, and it's premature until something survives this.
